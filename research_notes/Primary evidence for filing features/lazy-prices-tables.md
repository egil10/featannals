## What portfolio did Cohen, Malloy, and Nguyen actually publish?

### Takeaway
The broad result is a quintile long-short on year-over-year filing similarity for all EDGAR 10-Ks and 10-Qs linked to CRSP over 1995–2014: long non-changers (Q5), short changers (Q1), formed the month after the filing is public and held three months. There is no CAPM alpha in the tables. Value-weighted long-short alphas are about 34 to 58 basis points a month, depending on the similarity measure; the 58 basis-point cell (t = 3.59) is the simple side-by-side measure’s Fama-French three-factor alpha, not a five-factor alpha and not Jaccard.

### Cited Findings
- Sample and documents: “all complete 10-K, 10-K405, 10-KSB and 10-Q filings” from EDGAR, 1995 to 2014. Tables, exhibits, XBRL, and binary files are stripped. Monthly returns are from CRSP; book equity and earnings per share from Compustat; analyst data from I/B/E/S. Table I counts 86,965 10-Ks (mean 44,509 words) and 258,271 10-Qs (mean 15,806 words). Similarity is computed on 327,130 filings. The authors call this “the universe of firms (not a small, illiquid or otherwise selected subset).” No NYSE/AMEX/NASDAQ share-code screen, price screen, or size screen is stated in the data section that was read. — [NBER w25084, revised March 2019](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w25084/w25084.pdf)
- Comparison window: a 10-K is compared with the prior year’s 10-K; a 10-Q is compared with the same quarter’s 10-Q in the prior year, not with the immediately preceding quarter. — [NBER w25084](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w25084/w25084.pdf)
- Four measures, none labeled the single main specification. Cosine uses term frequency. Jaccard is binary (each word once): size of the intersection divided by the size of the union. Minimum edit distance counts insertions, deletions, and substitutions. Sim_Simple is a side-by-side diff: (additions + deletions + changes) divided by average document size, then rescaled so that 1 means identical. Figure 7’s event-time plot uses Jaccard. Means (Table I, Panel B): cosine 0.872, Jaccard 0.395, minimum edit 0.376, simple 0.146. — [NBER w25084](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w25084/w25084.pdf)
- Sort and timing, as printed in the Table II caption: quintiles, not deciles, “based on the prior year’s distribution of similarity measures across all stocks.” Stocks “enter the quintile portfolios in the month after the public release” and “are held in the portfolio for 3 months.” The body also says portfolios are rebalanced monthly, so the three-month hold overlaps. One sentence in the body instead says quintiles are computed “each month based on the prior month’s distribution.” The table that contains the returns says prior year. — [NBER w25084](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w25084/w25084.pdf)
- Factors actually in the portfolio tables: excess return is return minus the risk-free rate, not a CAPM alpha. No market-model alpha column was found. The three-factor alpha is market, size, and value. The five-factor alpha is market, size, value, momentum, and liquidity. Appendix Table A-1 names the loadings MKTRF, SMB, HML, UMD, and PS_VWF (Pastor-Stambaugh). This is not the Fama-French five-factor model. Profitability and investment appear only later, as Fama-MacBeth controls in Table XI, not as a portfolio alpha. — [NBER w25084](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w25084/w25084.pdf); [appendix](https://back.nber.org/appendix/w25084/w25084.appendix.pdf)
- Table II long-short (Q5 minus Q1), returns ×100, so the printed number is percent per month. Equal-weighted. Cosine: excess 0.31 (t = 3.13), three-factor 0.34 (t = 4.45), five-factor 0.32 (t = 4.21). Jaccard: excess 0.38 (t = 2.65), three-factor 0.44 (t = 4.56), five-factor 0.42 (t = 4.31). Minimum edit: excess 0.36 (t = 2.69), three-factor 0.48 (t = 5.96), five-factor 0.45 (t = 5.46). Simple: excess 0.18 (t = 1.20, not starred), three-factor 0.28 (t = 3.22), five-factor 0.27 (t = 3.01). The body’s “18–45 basis points per month” matches this equal-weighted range. — [NBER w25084](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w25084/w25084.pdf). The September 2018 revision prints the same cells in decimal (Jaccard equal-weighted excess 0.0038, t = 2.65; five-factor 0.0042, t = 4.31). — [w25084.rev0](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w25084/revisions/w25084.rev0.pdf)
- Table II, value-weighted Q5 minus Q1, percent per month. Cosine: excess 0.34 (t = 2.53), three-factor 0.35 (t = 2.63), five-factor 0.34 (t = 2.53). Jaccard: excess 0.56 (t = 3.75), three-factor 0.54 (t = 4.08), five-factor 0.46 (t = 3.44). Minimum edit: excess 0.39 (t = 2.31), three-factor 0.46 (t = 3.06), five-factor 0.37 (t = 2.45). Simple: excess 0.50 (t = 2.69), three-factor 0.58 (t = 3.59), five-factor 0.51 (t = 3.14). The introduction’s “34–58 basis points per month — up to 7% per year (t=3.59)” is this value-weighted band. The 58 basis points and the t-statistic 3.59 are the simple measure’s three-factor alpha. Twelve times 0.58 percent is about 7.0 percent; that is a monthly alpha times twelve, not a reported one-year buy-and-hold. — [NBER w25084](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w25084/w25084.pdf)
- Both legs contribute in the value-weighted panel. For Jaccard, Q1 excess is 0.23 and Q5 excess is 0.79; the three-factor alphas are −0.32 (t = −2.97) and +0.23 (t = 2.01). Table III, market value of equity in thousands of dollars: Q1 (changers) 3,507,587 versus Q5 (non-changers) 2,464,603, i.e. about $3.51 billion versus $2.46 billion. Shorting fees are 71.7 basis points in Q1 and 73.5 basis points in Q5. — [NBER w25084](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w25084/w25084.pdf)
- The introduction says the returns “continue to accrue out to 18 months, and do not reverse.” The only event-time window the text actually describes is Figure 7, Jaccard, “1 month out to 6 months after portfolio formation,” with “no statistically or economically detectable effect” from t−10 to t+10 days around the filing. — [NBER w25084](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w25084/w25084.pdf)
- Table XI, Fama-MacBeth, return ×100, with size, book-to-market, past returns, investment (capx/ppent), gross profitability, accruals, free cash flow, and a Compustat earnings surprise: Jaccard coefficient 0.55 (t = 4.20). That is a slope, not a portfolio alpha, and the factor model is still not a portfolio five-factor regression on profitability and investment. — [NBER w25084](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w25084/w25084.pdf)

### Inferences
- A reader who wants one number for the broad academic sort should quote the value-weighted band, and should name the cell: up to 58 basis points a month is Sim_Simple, value-weighted, three-factor alpha, t = 3.59. Jaccard’s value-weighted five-factor alpha is smaller, 46 basis points (t = 3.44). Equal-weighted five-factor alphas run from 27 basis points (simple) to 45 basis points (minimum edit).
- “Over the following year” in the introduction is not the holding period. The holding period in Table II is three months.

### Gaps
- No CAPM alpha was found. Do not fill one in.
- The typeset Journal of Finance tables were not opened. If copy-editing changed a cell between the March 2019 manuscript and the June 2020 issue, that change was not checked.
- No exchange, share-code, or minimum-price filter was found. Absence from the pages that were read is not proof the CRSP merge had none.
- The 18-month accrual is a sentence. No 18-month table was found. The figure the paper describes stops at six months.

## Is 188 basis points a table cell?

### Takeaway
Yes. It is one cell, and the abstract’s “up to” is doing real work. Equal-weighted Jaccard similarity of the risk-factor section, five-factor alpha, Q5 minus Q1, is 0.0188 per month (t = 2.76) in the September 2018 Table VII. That is 188 basis points a month, which is what “over 22% per year” is (12 × 1.88%). It is not the broad sort.

### Cited Findings
- Abstract, NBER and the journal landing pages: a portfolio that shorts changers and buys non-changers “earns up to 188 basis points” in monthly alpha, “over 22% per year.” The HBS wording is “up to 188 basis points per month (over 22% per year) in abnormal returns.” — [NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w25084); [Wiley](https://doi.org/10.1111/jofi.12885); [HBS](https://www.hbs.edu/faculty/Pages/item.aspx?num=57331)

- The cell, September 2018 Table VII, Panel A (equal-weighted), Risk Factors row, Sim_Jaccard, five-factor alpha: 0.0188*** with t = 2.7601. Same row, same measure: excess return 0.0143 (t = 2.13), three-factor alpha 0.0144 (t = 2.45). Cosine on that row is not significant (five-factor 0.0118, t = 1.64). ## Additions versus deletions, positive language, and 10-Q versus 10-K

### Takeaway
There is no additions-versus-deletions portfolio and no portfolio that computes similarity on positive words only. Positive sentiment of the change predicts higher returns in a regression, with the opposite sign of unsigned document change. Separating 10-Qs from 10-Ks does not remove the result; the value-weighted 10-K-only cosine spread is larger than the 10-Q-only spread, on a longer hold.

### Cited Findings
- Additions and deletions are summed, not traded separately. Sim_Simple’s numerator is “additions + deletions + changes,” divided by average document size. No long-short that isolates additions from deletions was found. — [NBER w25084](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w25084/w25084.pdf)
- Sign of the change, in prose only: “86% of changes consist of negative sentiment changes. When we segregate out the 14% of changes that are positive changes, we do find that they predict significantly positive returns in the future.” No portfolio mean, alpha, or t-statistic is attached to that 14 percent in the paragraph. — [NBER w25084](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w25084/w25084.pdf); same sentence in [w25084.rev0](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w25084/revisions/w25084.rev0.pdf)
- Table VI is a Fama-MacBeth regression, not a positive-language similarity sort. “Sentiment of Change is Positive” is the number of positive words in the change, normalized by the size of the change. With return multiplied by 100, the coefficient is 0.21 (t = 4.33) beside Jaccard 0.58 (t = 3.82). The September 2018 file prints the same regression in decimal: 0.0021 (t = 4.33) and Jaccard 0.0058 (t = 3.82). A higher positive-word share of the edit lines up with higher future returns. Jaccard stays positive and significant in the same column. This is not year-over-year similarity computed inside the positive dictionary. — [NBER w25084, Table VI](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w25084/w25084.pdf); [w25084.rev0, Table VI](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w25084/revisions/w25084.rev0.pdf)
- Appendix Table A-9 compares Jaccard with tone change of the whole report, Loughran-McDonald lists, Fama-MacBeth, decimal returns. Jaccard remains 0.0057 to 0.0060 (t from 3.26 to 3.48). Negative tone change is −0.0029 (t = −5.83) in the specification that also includes Jaccard. Positive tone change is +0.0018 (t = 3.58) in that same column. Again a regression coefficient, and again the tone of the whole document, not a positive-word overlap score. — [appendix Table A-9](https://back.nber.org/appendix/w25084/w25084.appendix.pdf)
- Definition drift between drafts: the September 2018 Table I text defines Sentiment of Change as positive words minus negative words in the change. The March 2019 Table I text defines Sentiment of Change as the number of negative words in the change, normalized by the size of the change. The portfolio tables do not sort on either definition. — [w25084.rev0](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w25084/revisions/w25084.rev0.pdf); [w25084 revised March 2019](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w25084/w25084.pdf)
- 10-Q versus 10-K, Appendix Table A-13, cosine only, quintiles on the prior year’s distribution, decimal returns. 10-Q only, held three months. Equal-weighted Q5 minus Q1: excess 0.0038 (t = 4.12), three-factor 0.0049 (t = 6.91), five-factor 0.0049 (t = 6.80). Value-weighted: excess 0.0031 (t = 2.29), three-factor 0.0036 (t = 2.61), five-factor 0.0035 (t = 2.50). — [appendix Table A-13, Panels A1 and A2](https://back.nber.org/appendix/w25084/w25084.appendix.pdf)

### Inferences
- A later backtest that flips the sign by scoring only positive-word overlap is not a Cohen, Malloy, and Nguyen portfolio. Their signed result is a regression slope on the sentiment of the edit, and it does not overturn the unsigned-similarity long-short.
- Quote 10-K and 10-Q results from Table A-13 separately, and quote the nine-month 10-K hold whenever the 68 basis-point value-weighted five-factor number is used. Do not describe that cell as the three-month Table II design.

- No mean return or alpha for the 14 percent “positive changes” subsample was found. The sentence says the sign flips and stops there.
- Table A-13 is cosine only. Jaccard and simple similarity were not broken out by 10-K versus 10-Q in the appendix pages that were read.

## What did Research Affiliates test in June 2026, and is 2.9 percent Cohen’s portfolio?

### Takeaway
The 2.9 percent figure is real, and it is not a replication of Cohen, Malloy, and Nguyen’s long-short. Que Nguyen and Joseph Shim, not Quoc Nguyen, sort the 1,000 largest U.S. stocks into three cap-weighted Jaccard buckets from April 1995 through September 2025. Steady-language stocks beat the universe by 1.4 percent a year and big changers lag it by 1.5 percent. The October 2015–September 2025 slice is wider, a 4.0 percent excess spread, not a decay.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- This is a related construction, not a replication. It keeps Jaccard, the year-over-year same-quarter comparison, the long-non-changers / short-changers sign, and the absence of a filing-day jump. It changes the universe (top 1,000, not all filers), the sort (terciles, not quintiles), the weights (cap weight only), the rebalance (quarterly on the latest score, or annual, not a three-month overlapping hold), the factor model (Carhart four, not market-size-value-momentum-liquidity), and the reported object (excess return versus the large-cap universe, not a monthly long-short alpha).
- The post-2014 number an editor can put next to “the anomaly decayed after publication” is Exhibit 9’s October 2015–September 2025 excess spread of 4.0 percent (−2.4 versus +1.7), which is wider than either earlier decade in that exhibit. That is still one firm’s gross-of-cost simulation.

### Gaps
- Research Affiliates do not report a single long-short alpha, a t-statistic on the 2.9 percent spread, or a five-factor alpha with profitability and investment. Do not construct one by subtracting −1.49 from +1.76.
- They do not split 10-K from 10-Q, and they do not report section-level Jaccard. Part 2 of the series is announced and was not in this PDF.
- No live Research Affiliates HTML article was opened beyond the PDF and the Advisor Perspectives author index. The PDF’s own URL line is researchaffiliates.com; the file used here is the Strapi media link.

## Which number should a liquid U.S. book underwrite?

### Takeaway
Underwrite about 2.9 percentage points a year of gross excess-return spread inside the 1,000 largest U.S. stocks, cap-weighted Jaccard terciles, quarterly rebalance, April 1995–September 2025, and treat the October 2015–September 2025 print as wider (4.0 percentage points) rather than as decay. Treat 188 basis points a month as the in-sample tail: equal-weighted, risk-factor text only, Jaccard, five-factor alpha, full filer universe, 1995–2014, t = 2.76.

### Cited Findings
- The liquid-book object that was actually printed is Research Affiliates Exhibit 2: Bucket 3 excess +1.4 percent and Bucket 1 excess −1.5 percent, spread 2.9 percent, top 1,000, cap-weighted, quarterly, through September 2025, gross of costs. Annual rebalancing, Exhibit 10, cuts the excesses to +1.1 and −1.2 percent. The four-factor leg alphas on the quarterly book are −1.49 percent (t = −2.29) and +1.76 percent (t = 2.59), reported separately. — [RA PDF, Exhibits 2, 3, and 10](https://loved-novelty-f15595edb6.media.strapiapp.com/1117_nlp_for_long_horizon_factor_investing_d39e26f476.pdf)
- The academic broad sort, which still includes every CRSP-linked filer and is not a top-1,000 book, is the value-weighted Table II band of 34 to 58 basis points a month. The top of that band is Sim_Simple’s three-factor alpha, 0.58 percent (t = 3.59). Jaccard’s value-weighted five-factor alpha is 0.46 percent (t = 3.44). — [NBER w25084, Table II](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w25084/w25084.pdf)
- The 188 basis-point number is the September 2018 Table VII cell specified above, and the abstract says “up to.” The same section after 2002, equal-weighted Jaccard, is 111 basis points a month of excess return (t = 3.15), still not a large-cap tercile. — [w25084.rev0, Table VII](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w25084/revisions/w25084.rev0.pdf); [appendix Table A-1](https://back.nber.org/appendix/w25084/w25084.appendix.pdf)

### Inferences
- Do not average 188 basis points with 2.9 percent. One is a monthly equal-weighted alpha on a parsed risk-factor section in the academic universe. The other is an annual excess-return gap between the top and bottom third of the large-cap universe against that universe’s own return. A practitioner who wants the Cohen specification closest to a liquid book, and is willing to leave the top-1,000 test aside, still does not get 188: the value-weighted full-document sort is the 34–58 basis-point band, and even that is all filers.
- Inside the Research Affiliates large-cap book, the spread they print is larger among names that are already cheap, unprofitable, aggressively investing, volatile, or low momentum (3.5 to 5.3 percent, and 3.8 percent inside cheap stocks: 12.3 versus 8.5). That is a conditional use of the same signal, not a second estimate of the unconditional 2.9.

### Gaps
- Neither paper states a net-of-cost return for a top-1,000 implementation of Cohen’s exact quintile, three-month-hold design. Research Affiliates’ 2.9 percent is the closest primary number, and it is a different portfolio, gross of costs, from one firm’s simulation.
- No Journal of Finance table was read, so a copy-edit of the 188 cell in the June 2020 issue remains unchecked. The claim is in the journal abstract, the March 2019 manuscript, and the September 2018 Table VII cell 0.0188 (t = 2.76).
