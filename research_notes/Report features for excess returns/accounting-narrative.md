# Accounting and footnote narrative features for future excess returns

Scope is US GAAP / SEC filings (10-K, 10-Q, earnings releases furnished under 8-K Item 2.02), with IFRS note analogues where the same disclosure exists. This is an evidence catalogue, not a shortlist. A claim is tagged **returns** only when a source reports post-disclosure stock returns; **fundamentals-only** when the source stops at earnings, cash flows, bankruptcy, or liquidity events; **short-window** when the source is a filing-date or announcement reaction rather than a subsequent holding-period anomaly. Magnitudes are quoted only when the source states them. Where a later paper attributes a number to an earlier paper, that is labeled secondary.

Publication and snooping prior that applies to every cluster: across 97 cross-sectional predictors, portfolio returns are 26% lower out of sample and 58% lower after publication; the gap (about 32%) is attributed to publication-informed trading. Declines are larger for predictors with higher in-sample returns, and surviving returns concentrate in high-idiosyncratic-risk, low-liquidity stocks. — [McLean and Pontiff, Journal of Finance, 2016](https://doi.org/10.1111/jofi.12365)

## Which narrative features have return evidence, and where do they live?

### Takeaway

The features with the cleanest post-disclosure **return** evidence that a reader gets from prose, rather than from a Compustat ratio, are year-over-year document changes (especially MD&A, litigation, risk-factor, and executive-team language), MD&A tone *changes* and abnormal tone in earnings releases, the composition of non-GAAP exclusions, material-weakness disclosures, and a subset of going-concern cases. Accruals, Beneish, Piotroski, asset growth, capex, pension funded status, goodwill balances, backlog levels, and deferred-revenue changes are mostly **numeric** anomalies that happen to be discussed in MD&A; the prose is not what the trading tests used. Several popular red flags (RPO/cRPO, net retention, price-versus-volume, lease footnotes, warranty text, auditor changes, qualitative goodwill “headroom”) had no return magnitude located in this pass and should not be promoted on earnings-quality intuition alone.

### Cited Findings

#### How to read a filing (stable extraction targets)

- 10-K narrative homes: Item 1 Business (Regulation S-K Item 101, including major customers), Item 1A Risk Factors (Item 105), Item 3 Legal Proceedings (Item 103), Item 7 MD&A (Item 303), Item 7A market risk (Item 305), Item 8 financial statements and notes, Item 9A controls (SOX 404). Customer concentration used in the return papers is the SFAS 131 / ASC 280 fact that a customer is more than 10% of revenue, which is also an Item 101 disclosure. — [Patatoukas SSRN abstract, citing SFAS 131](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=1666497); [customer-concentration crash-risk paper, ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0148296320300618)
- 10-Q homes: Part I Item 1 condensed financials and notes, Item 2 MD&A, Item 4 controls (SOX 302); Part II Item 1 legal proceedings and Item 1A material risk-factor updates. Tone-change return tests explicitly use the MD&A of both 10-Q and 10-K. — [Feldman, Govindaraj, Livnat, and Segal, Review of Accounting Studies, 2010](https://ideas.repec.org/a/spr/reaccs/v15y2010i4d10.1007_s11142-009-9111-x.html)
- Non-GAAP addbacks are tabulated first in the earnings release furnished under 8-K Item 2.02 (Exhibit 99.1), under Regulation G. The academic exclusion tests are built from that earnings-announcement setting, not from a once-a-year 10-K. — [Doyle, Lundholm, and Soliman, Review of Accounting Studies, 2003, as summarized by Michigan Ross](https://www.bus.umich.edu/FacultyResearch/InTheNews/Archive/ProFormaEarnings.htm); [SSRN abstract](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=303563)
- Note-level targets an LLM can point at, whether or not returns exist: ASC 606 remaining performance obligations (revenue note; public companies, including interim), ASC 450 loss contingencies (legal note and Item 3), ASC 715 pension footnote, ASC 740 unrecognized tax benefits, ASC 718 stock-based compensation, ASC 842 leases, ASC 350 goodwill. RPO is the transaction price allocated to unsatisfied obligations, disclosed with timing; the one-year practical expedient and non-public relief mean coverage is incomplete. IFRS 15 paragraph 120 is the analogue. — [ASC 606-10-50-13 summary](https://www.scalexp.com/saas-metrics-library/remaining-performance-obligation-rpo/); [RPO versus deferred revenue](https://www.fiscallion.io/blog/remaining-performance-obligations-saas)
- IFRS annual-report analogues of MD&A are the strategic report / management commentary and, where used, a separate risk report. IAS 37 tracks ASC 450; IAS 19 tracks pensions, but post-revision IAS 19 does not run an expected-return-on-assets earnings assumption the way US GAAP net periodic pension cost still does; IAS 36 asks for goodwill-impairment assumptions and sensitivity (closer to “headroom” prose than US GAAP); IFRIC 23 tracks uncertain tax; IFRS 16 tracks leases; IAS 1 is the going-concern statement. No IFRS-specific return magnitude for these narratives was located in this pass. Lang and Stice-Lawrence-style international annual-report text was not re-opened here (see Gaps).

#### 1. Accruals and earnings-quality language

- Sloan-style accruals are a **numeric** anomaly. This pass did not re-extract the original hedge magnitude from Sloan (1996, The Accounting Review). Treat published accrual spreads as subject to the McLean–Pontiff decay prior above, not as a prose feature. — [McLean and Pontiff](https://doi.org/10.1111/jofi.12365)
- What prose adds, with a return interaction: average tone of MD&A forward-looking statements (naive Bayes, not a dictionary) is positively associated with future earnings after other determinants. Dictionary tone (Diction, General Inquirer, LIWC) does **not** positively predict future performance. When MD&A tone “warns” in the opposite direction of accruals — positive tone with negative accruals, or negative tone with positive accruals — accruals are not associated with future returns. That is a returns result, but it is an interaction that shuts off mispricing, not a standalone tone portfolio magnitude. Sample is 10-K and 10-Q MD&A. Mechanism in the paper: information about future earnings, i.e. a fundamentals channel that the market otherwise misprices via accruals. — [Li, Journal of Accounting Research, 2010, SSRN abstract](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=1623943)
- Beneish M-score is eight accounting ratios (receivables, gross margin, asset quality, sales growth, depreciation, SG&A, leverage, accruals), not a narrative score. Flagged firms earned one-year-ahead size-adjusted returns of −7.5% versus +2.4% for unflagged firms. Risk-adjusted underperformance averages just below 1% per month. Inside the lowest accrual quintile, the high-minus-low M-score size-adjusted spread is −19.8% over the next 12 months (−10.5% in the second-lowest accrual quintile). The paper’s interpretation is that M-score forecasts the reversal of accruals, and is most useful precisely where accruals look high-quality. Mechanism: mispricing / expected-return difference from manipulation risk, not claimed as a risk premium. An LLM that narrates DSO, capitalization, or margin language is a substitute extraction path for inputs Compustat already has; this paper does not show the sentences beat the ratios. — [Beneish, Lee, and Nichols, Financial Analysts Journal, 2013 (PDF)](https://cpb-us-w2.wpmucdn.com/sites.udel.edu/dist/a/855/files/2020/07/Earnings-Manipulation-and-Expected-Returns.pdf)
- Piotroski F-score is nine numeric signals (profitability, cash versus earnings, ΔROA, leverage, liquidity, dilution, gross margin, asset turnover). A secondary explainer attributes to the 2000 Journal of Accounting Research paper an improvement of the high book-to-market strategy from selecting financially strong firms; the same page words this as high versus low F-score outperformance of 7.5% a year, which may compress the original wording. A separate 1997–2024 US value backtest finds score 8–9 CAGR of 6.1% versus 9.4% for the S&P 500, and decade spreads versus score 0–2 that flip sign. Use the original paper only as a numeric value-investing control, and treat post-2000 performance as decayed or implementation-sensitive. Do not promote MD&A sentences that restate F-score components as an incremental return feature. — [GuruScreens summary of Piotroski 2000](https://guruscreens.com/blog/piotroski-f-score-explained); [Trading Studio 1997–2024 backtest](https://blog.tradingstudio.finance/piotroski-f-score-backtest-us-stocks/)
- Phrase-level forensic flags are weaker than the ratios. In a large 10-K sample, “unbilled receivables” is associated with subsequent fraud accusations (**fundamentals / fraud**, not a holding-period return). “Substantial doubt” is associated with lower **filing-date** excess returns, higher volatility, and wider analyst forecast dispersion (**short-window**, not a demonstrated multi-month alpha in that paper). — [Loughran and McDonald, Journal of Behavioral Finance, 2011](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=1510188)
- Material-weakness **quality** link, not returns: weaknesses are associated with accruals that do not map into cash, and that link is driven by company-level controls rather than account-specific weaknesses. Label **fundamentals-only**. The return evidence is in cluster 5. — [Doyle, Ge, and McVay, The Accounting Review, 2007 (archived PDF)](https://web.archive.org/web/20100611220053if_/http:/www.business.utah.edu/humis/docs/person_1373_1193775147.pdf)
- LLM extraction that the literature says beats keyword counts: classify forward-looking MD&A sentences and score their tone with a trained or contextual model; do not use raw positive/negative dictionaries. Li shows the dictionaries fail and the learned measure both predicts earnings and conditions the accrual return. — [Li 2010 SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=1623943)

#### 2. Non-GAAP exclusions and adjusted-EBITDA addbacks

- Higher exclusions from pro forma earnings predict lower future cash flows. Investors price some of this at the announcement, but not enough. A long-low-exclusion / short-high-exclusion strategy earns a large positive abnormal return over the following years after risk factors and other anomalies. The institution summary of the same study says stock returns over the three years after the announcement are up to 45% lower for relatively large exclusions than for small exclusions. Special items (for example a restructuring) predict about one more dollar of future cash flow per dollar excluded; “other exclusions” predict about three fewer dollars of cash flow over three years. So the red flag is recurring cash costs labeled non-recurring, not every addback. Mechanism: underreaction (drift), not a claimed risk premium. Venue: quarterly earnings announcements, i.e. 8-K Item 2.02, sample era 1988–1999, before Regulation G. — [Michigan Ross summary](https://www.bus.umich.edu/FacultyResearch/InTheNews/Archive/ProFormaEarnings.htm); [RAS 2003 abstract](https://ideas.repec.org/a/spr/reaccs/v8y2003i2d10.1023_a1024472210359.html)
- A follow-on valuation paper agrees that exclusions are persistent and that stocks with positive “other exclusions” look overpriced, but argues the pricing error is sometimes the opposite sign of a simple underreaction story. Do not treat the 45% figure as uncontested in mechanism. — [Scispace PDF discussing Doyle, Lundholm, and Soliman](https://scispace.com/pdf/implications-of-components-of-income-excluded-from-pro-forma-46n07qn1nu.pdf)
- Post-Reg G update located in this pass does **not** reconfirm a negative return to exclusions. Firms that consistently report non-GAAP metrics have investments associated with similar or higher future cash flows than GAAP-only reporters, and the tests include future returns and earnings as outcome variables in that investment-performance design. That is evidence against “non-GAAP firm = overinvestment,” not a replication of the exclusion hedge. — [Journal of Accounting and Economics, 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0165410124000909)
- LLM extraction versus a keyword count of “adjusted EBITDA”: read the reconciliation and classify each line into special item versus other exclusion, cash versus non-cash, and one-time versus recurring (stock-based compensation, “transformation” costs, acquisition retention, amortization). The return result attaches to the **amount and type** of the exclusion, which is a number in a table. The incremental value of prose is the classification Compustat special items do not make. No paper located here shows that softer adjectives around the table (“record,” “underlying”) predict returns after the exclusion amount.

#### 3. Revenue-quality narrative

- Order-backlog **level** (backlog scaled by operations): the market overweights backlog’s contribution to future earnings. A hedge on current backlog earns significant future abnormal returns. Analysts are not the source of the mispricing. Magnitude is not stated in the abstract. Disclosure is voluntary in form and uneven; Compustat order backlog is the input, so this is numeric, with the narrative in Item 1 / MD&A as a coverage backfill when the tag is missing. Mechanism: mispricing (overweighting). — [Rajgopal, Shevlin, and Venkatachalam, Review of Accounting Studies, 2003](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1023/A:1027364031775)
- Order-backlog **changes** have the opposite pricing error. A long-high-change / short-low-change decile hedge earns 13.7% in the following year (sample described as 32,738 firm-years, 1971–1999). Analysts are overoptimistic when backlog falls. The market underweights changes, in contrast to overweighting levels. Mechanism: drift / underreaction. This is the cleanest “changes beat levels” revenue result in the pass. Authors are not named on the extracted pages; cite the document rather than a guessed byline. — [Scispace PDF, “Changes in order backlog and future returns”](https://scispace.com/pdf/changes-in-order-backlog-and-future-returns-8l5w7mlk5r.pdf)
- Deferred-revenue **changes**: US evidence (Prakash and Sinha, Contemporary Accounting Research, 2013) is that deferred-revenue changes are a leading indicator and that investors misprice them because revenue and expense recognition are mismatched. This pass did not extract a US portfolio return from the primary paper. Indian and Chinese replications find hedge returns to high-minus-low deferred-revenue changes; those are not US magnitudes. Compustat deferred revenue makes the US test numeric. Narrative billings, duration, and cancellation terms were not separately return-tested here. — [Prakash and Sinha, CAR, 2013](http://dx.doi.org/10.1111/j.1911-3846.2012.01164.x); [Indian replication summarizing the US mispricing claim](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1061951821000665)
- Customer-base concentration **changes**: increases predict efficiency gains and one-year-ahead stock returns; a trading strategy on the underreaction earns abnormal returns over roughly thirty years. In the authors’ slides the one-year-ahead return regression coefficient on the change is about 0.10 with size and other controls (do not convert that coefficient into a portfolio return without their scaling). Sign is **positive** on increases in concentration. Mechanism they argue: underreaction, because a disproportionate share of the effect is around later earnings announcements and it varies with analyst following and institutional ownership. — [Patatoukas SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=1666497); [author slides](https://pdfs.semanticscholar.org/presentation/7d4b/a069dd746f61c91f25e87457b802d155799e.pdf)
- Customer concentration **levels** point the other way, and look like risk rather than a red-flag short. Firms that disclose a major customer (more than 10% of revenue) have lower excess returns than firms that do not, including after standard factors; given that indicator, further concentration does not change excess returns. The authors read this as lower systematic risk, stronger for small, young, and thinly covered firms and in recessions. Sorts run January 1985–December 2022. A separate paper finds corporate (not government) customer concentration is positively associated with crash risk, which is a tail outcome, not a mean-return anomaly. — [Bauer working PDF](https://www.bauer.uh.edu/yerramilli/CustomerConcentration.pdf); [crash-risk paper](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0148296320300618)
- Downgraded, no return paper located: organic versus acquired growth, price versus volume versus mix, recurring versus one-time revenue, bookings versus revenue, cancellation language, net retention, and churn. Item 303 asks managers to explain material revenue changes, so an LLM can extract these, but that is not return evidence. Remaining performance obligations and current RPO are required ASC 606 disclosures in the revenue note and are widely repeated in MD&A and the earnings release; no academic post-disclosure abnormal-return magnitude was found. Do not infer an RPO drift from the backlog or deferred-revenue results. RPO excludes cancellable periods and contracts with an original duration of one year or less, so it is not bookings.

#### 4. Investment, issuance, and capital allocation

- Realized asset growth and abnormal capex are the Compustat benchmarks in this cluster (Cooper, Gulen, and Schill; Titman, Wei, and Xie). This pass did not re-open those papers, so no magnitude is stated. They are numeric. MD&A sentences about capex plans were not shown, in sources read here, to predict returns after realized asset growth. Downgrade plan-language until that incremental test exists. — decay prior only: [McLean and Pontiff](https://doi.org/10.1111/jofi.12365)
- Covenant-violation **text** is a genuine filing feature: Compustat does not flag it cleanly, and the papers read SEC filings. In any year, about 10% to 20% of US nonfinancial firms (1996–2008) report a financial-covenant violation. Violations are followed by cuts to acquisitions, capex, leverage, and payouts, higher CEO turnover, and **improved** operating and stock-price performance. Sign for subsequent returns is positive, not a distress short. Mechanism: creditor intervention that raises value, not underreaction to bad news. A related paper finds new capex restrictions after violations line up with higher market value and ROA. — [Nini, Sufi, and Smith, SSRN, December 2011](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=1344302); [Nini, Smith, and Sufi, Journal of Financial Economics, 2009](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0304405X09000233)
- Constraining words in the 10-K (a lexicon, not a generic negative list) have low correlation with size/age constraint indexes and predict dividend omissions or increases, equity recycling, and underfunded pensions better than those indexes. That is **fundamentals / liquidity events**, not a published return alpha in the abstract. — [Bodnaruk, Loughran, and McDonald, Journal of Financial and Quantitative Analysis, 2015](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2331544)
- Stock-based compensation, shelf registration, and buyback credibility: no standalone return paper was located. SBC matters in this catalogue only as a candidate “other exclusion” inside the Doyle–Lundholm–Soliman split. Keyword counts of “repurchase” or “accretive” are not evidence.

#### 5. Distress, liquidity, covenants, going concern, material weaknesses, restatements, auditor changes

- Material weakness under SOX 302 (2007–2023): announcement reaction is negligible, then a negative drift over the next two quarters. Buy-and-hold abnormal returns are about 3% over 60 trading days and 6% over 120 trading days; the gap versus effective-control firms is about 5.3%–6.2% (about 11%–12% annualized). The same summary also describes roughly 10%–16% annualized underperformance. The drift survives risk models, post-earnings-announcement drift, accruals, momentum, distress, and penny stocks, and is confined to low institutional ownership. Authors’ mechanism: investors underreact to persistence (they cite a 79% conditional probability of a subsequent material weakness versus a 6% unconditional probability). This is drift, not a risk premium. Location: 10-Q Item 4 and the 10-K controls section, not the earnings press release. LLM task versus a keyword: detect an actual weakness conclusion, and separate company-level from account-specific, because only company-level weaknesses map into accruals quality in Doyle, Ge, and McVay (fundamentals). The drift paper’s snippet does not show that split. — [Ghosh and coauthors, Journal of Business Finance & Accounting, 2026](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jbfa.70012); [Doyle, Ge, and McVay 2007](https://web.archive.org/web/20100611220053if_/http:/www.business.utah.edu/humis/docs/person_1373_1193775147.pdf)
- Going concern, United Kingdom: in the year after disclosure, firms underperform by 24% to 31% depending on the benchmark (raw returns about −17% in a rising market). Not PEAD, penny stocks, distress status, or momentum in that paper’s tests. First-time emphasis in later citations. This is a non-US return result. — [Taffler, Lu, and Kausar, Journal of Accounting and Economics, 2004](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0165410104000813)
- Going concern, United States, does **not** cleanly replicate. A US and Australia study finds no significant negative abnormal returns in Australia, and in the US the negative returns disappear under factor models or after momentum. — [Journal of Accounting and Economics, 2007](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0165410107000250)
- A 2026 paper argues the US underreaction is a lottery-stock phenomenon. Lottery-like going-concern stocks underperform by −17% to −32% over the next year. Five-factor 12-month post-announcement abnormal return is −10.6% for the full going-concern sample, −25.8% for lottery-like names, and a non-significant −4.8% for the rest. Lottery-like names underperform other going-concern stocks by up to −21%. The paper cites Kausar, Taffler, and Tan (2009) at about −14% over 12 months after a first-time going-concern report, and another source notes that transaction costs limit the trade. Mechanism: retail gambling plus slow incorporation of distress, i.e. drift concentrated where arbitrage is hard, not a uniform risk premium. — [ScienceDirect, 2026](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1386418125000515)
- MD&A going-concern **sentences** and MD&A tone predict bankruptcy incremental to ratios, market variables, and the auditor’s going-concern opinion, including three years before bankruptcy. Less than 40% of bankrupt firms discuss going concern in the MD&A, and only about 40–50% receive an auditor going-concern opinion. This paper is **fundamentals-only** (bankruptcy). It is still the right extraction target if a later model maps bankruptcy probability into returns: an explicit “substantial doubt” or inability-to-continue sentence, plus tone, not a negative-word rate. Auditor opinion lives in the Item 8 report; management’s version lives in MD&A and sometimes the liquidity section. — [Mayew, Sethuraman, and Venkatachalam, The Accounting Review, 2015, SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2272463); [later paper quoting their hit rates](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1057/s41310-021-00107-3)
- “Substantial doubt” as a phrase: lower filing-date excess returns (**short-window** only in that paper). — [Loughran and McDonald 2011 behavioral-finance paper](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=1510188)
- Restatement language, auditor changes, and 8-K Item 4.02 non-reliance: no holding-period return magnitude was re-extracted in this pass. Do not borrow the material-weakness drift for restatements.

#### 6. Hidden liabilities

- Pension **numbers in the footnote**, not pension adjectives: the off-balance-sheet piece of funded status, and the PBO, predict future returns; the on-balance-sheet piece does not. Prices and analyst forecasts do not reflect the earnings effects of pension-footnote changes when the 10-K arrives; they catch up as later quarterly earnings show the effect. Mechanism: processing delay / drift. No return magnitude is in the abstract. Compustat pension items already include funded status and the expected-return assumption, so an LLM adds something only when it captures a change the database lags, or qualitative assumption language the paper did not test. — [Picconi, The Accounting Review, 2006](https://publications.aaahq.org/accounting-review/article/81/4/925/2885/The-Perils-of-Pensions-Does-Pension-Accounting)
- Severely underfunded sponsors earn lower stock returns than healthier plans for at least five years after underfunding appears. The original paper says the low returns are not explained by risk, price momentum, earnings momentum, or accruals, and that investors are surprised when the earnings effect arrives (drift). A later description of that result quotes a monthly four-factor alpha of 43 basis points (5.16% a year) over the succeeding five years; treat 43 basis points as secondary. The same later paper finds the predictability is concentrated in distressed sponsors and disappears once sponsor distress is controlled, and that it fades when underfunding comes from bad pension-asset returns or has lasted more than five years. Downgrade from “hidden liability alpha” to “partly a distress factor.” A related dynamic strategy is described at 1.51% per month for 1989–2004; that is a strategy paper, not the baseline sort. German evidence has the opposite sign, so the US result is institution-specific. — [Franzoni and Marín, Journal of Finance, 2006, working-paper abstract](https://ideas.repec.org/p/upf/upfgen/871.html); [Tao, Chen, Lu, and Zhang, International Review of Economics & Finance, 2017](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1059056016301174); [German counterexample](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0378426623000419)
- Goodwill **balances interacted with low profitability**, post-SFAS 142: impairments lag operating deterioration and stock returns by at least two years, and firms with high goodwill and low profitability have both more future impairments and lower future stock returns. Investors did not show the same misvaluation pattern before SFAS 142. Mechanism: delayed impairments and temporary overvaluation (drift). A blog implementation cites −15.6%; that number is not in the paper excerpt read here and should not be used. Qualitative “headroom” or “no impairment required” sentences were not the tested variable. A separate announcement study finds a negative short-window reaction to impairment news that is smaller after SFAS 142, and little reaction when a firm with impaired indicators reports zero impairment. — [Li and Sloan paper PDF](https://assets.super.so/e46b77e7-ee08-445e-b43f-4ffd88ae0a0e/files/e8ffe096-7515-43bc-a676-3316b43325b2.pdf); [blog gloss, do not cite as the paper’s magnitude](https://alphaarchitect.com/goodwill-gone-bad/); [Li, Shroff, Venkataraman, and Zhang SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=590908)
- Uncertain tax benefits: additions to the UTB are associated with less trading around the 10-K, wider subsequent spreads, and a longer post-disclosure drift, especially for tax avoiders, poor reporters, and low institutional ownership. Direction and size of the drift return are not quantified in the abstract. A 2026 paper, citing Robinson and Schmidt (2013), says UTBs are positively associated with abnormal returns and that more detailed reserve disclosure weakens that association. Treat the positive sign as a secondary citation, not a hidden-liability short. Pre-FIN 48, ETR increases and the reserve-driven piece of the ETR had negative announcement and 10-K filing returns; after FIN 48 those relations are not distinguishable from zero. — [Small and Song SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3815951); [RQFA 2026 PDF citing Robinson and Schmidt](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s11156-026-01492-y.pdf); [Wharton PDF on FIN 48 value relevance](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/RSS-20161024.pdf)
- Litigation **language changes** inside the periodic report predict returns, but only as part of the document-change result in cluster 7, not as a tested contingency-reserve multiple. No return magnitude was located for warranty reserves, lease footnotes, or off-balance-sheet arrangements as prose. ASC 842 moved operating leases onto the balance sheet, which shrinks the old off-balance-sheet lease signal; that institutional fact is not itself a return result.

#### 7. Placement, boilerplate, and tone

- Year-over-year **change** in 10-K and 10-Q language: a portfolio that shorts changers and buys non-changers earns up to 188 basis points per month in alpha (over 22% a year). The “up to” is the top of the authors’ specifications, not a median result, and is the number most exposed to the McLean–Pontiff warning on high in-sample returns. Changes are concentrated in the MD&A. Language tied to the CEO/CFO, to litigation, and (in one version of the abstract) to the risk-factor section is especially informative. There is no filing-date announcement effect; returns show up later through news, events, and earnings. Changes also predict earnings, profitability, news, and bankruptcy. Mechanism the authors argue: inattention, i.e. drift, not a risk premium and not an announcement underreaction of the usual kind. Sample of regular filings 1995–2014, published in the Journal of Finance in 2020, so post-publication decay is not inside the paper. — [Cohen, Malloy, and Nguyen, Journal of Finance, 2020](https://doi.org/10.1111/jofi.12885); [SSRN / NBER abstract with the section detail](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3254078); [HBS summary](https://www.hbs.edu/faculty/Pages/item.aspx?num=57331)
- MD&A modification score (how much the MD&A text changes): larger modifications line up with larger three-day absolute returns at the 10-K filing, not with analyst forecast revisions. Modification scores fell over the decade they study even as MD&As got longer, and the price reaction to modifications weakened. This is **short-window** informativeness, and it decayed inside the sample. It is not the same signed drift as Cohen, Malloy, and Nguyen: Brown and Tucker measure whether investors react at filing; Cohen, Malloy, and Nguyen measure who underperforms afterward (changers). — [Brown and Tucker, Journal of Accounting Research, 2011](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=1452977)
- 10-K complexity: there is a price and volume reaction around the filing, the immediate move lines up with future profitability, and a stock-price drift continues for 12 months. Underreaction is stronger when the 10-K is more complex. Magnitude of the drift is not in the abstract. Mechanism: processing costs / underreaction. This is the closest sourced result to “bad news is hard to find,” but the proxy is complexity, not a measured footnote-versus-front-page placement. — [You and Zhang, Review of Accounting Studies, 2009](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11142-008-9083-2)
- Domain negative word lists, not the Harvard dictionary: in 1994–2008 10-Ks, about 73.8% of Harvard “negative” counts are words that are not negative in financial text. The financial negative list is associated with filing-date returns, volume, later volatility, fraud, material weakness, and unexpected earnings. Generic keyword counts fail. This is mostly **short-window** plus fundamentals, not a clean long-horizon alpha in the abstract. — [Loughran and McDonald, Journal of Finance, 2011](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.2010.01625.x)
- Abnormal positive tone in earnings press releases, defined as the residual after quantitative performance, risk, and complexity: positive return at the earnings announcement, then a negative reaction over the next one and two quarters. Abnormal tone predicts negative future earnings and cash flows, and lines up with meet-or-beat, later restatements, seasoned equity offerings, and M&A. Mechanism: temporary misleading of investors (drift after a wrong-signed announcement reaction). This is the strongest “tone incremental to numbers” design, and it lives in the 8-K Item 2.02 release rather than the 10-K. No basis-point magnitude is in the abstract. Balance-sheet-constrained and older firms substitute tone management for accruals management. — [Huang, Teoh, and Zhang, The Accounting Review, 2014, SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=1960376)
- Tone dispersion (how evenly tone words are spread) is informative in conference-call narratives and is associated with analyst and investor responses. That is structure, not a 10-K footnote-burial test, and not a long-horizon filing hedge in the abstract. — [Allee and DeAngelis, Journal of Accounting Research, 2015](https://doi.org/10.1111/1475-679x.12072)
- Downgraded: a specific test that the same bad news earns a larger post-filing drift when it is placed in a footnote or exhibit rather than in MD&A was not found. Boilerplate-versus-firm-specific annual-report results in Lang and Stice-Lawrence were not re-read; Brown and Tucker already show that length rose while modification and the filing reaction fell.

#### 8. Changes versus levels

- The sourced return results that favor changes over levels, inside the same construct, are: backlog changes underweighted versus backlog levels overweighted; customer-concentration changes (positive drift) versus the level of having a major customer (lower expected returns, read as risk); MD&A tone **changes** incremental to earnings surprises and accruals; document changes rather than document length; pension **changes** in the footnote that are not in price at the 10-K date. Levels of negative words mostly show up as filing-date returns. — [backlog-change PDF](https://scispace.com/pdf/changes-in-order-backlog-and-future-returns-8l5w7mlk5r.pdf); [Rajgopal, Shevlin, and Venkatachalam](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1023/A:1027364031775); [Patatoukas](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=1666497); [Bauer PDF](https://www.bauer.uh.edu/yerramilli/CustomerConcentration.pdf); [Feldman et al.](https://ideas.repec.org/a/spr/reaccs/v15y2010i4d10.1007_s11142-009-9111-x.html); [Cohen, Malloy, and Nguyen](https://doi.org/10.1111/jofi.12885); [Brown and Tucker](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=1452977)

### Inferences

- A usable LLM feature is one the papers could not build from Compustat alone: semantic change in MD&A, litigation, risk factors, and named-executive discussion; residual tone in the earnings release; the recurring-versus-transitory split of non-GAAP lines; an explicit going-concern sentence; a SOX 302 weakness conclusion; a covenant-violation statement. Cosine similarity and word counts are the published instruments. An LLM should be more precise than those instruments, but that precision is not itself a published return.
- Sign is not uniformly “bad prose, low returns.” Non-changers outperform changers. Increases in customer concentration and increases in backlog have positive subsequent returns in the change papers. Covenant violations are followed by higher, not lower, stock-price performance. Abnormal optimism is bad; raw optimism that matches the numbers is not the same variable.
- The 188 basis-point Lazy Prices figure and the 45% three-year exclusion gap are the two largest magnitudes in this catalogue and the two most in need of a haircut: one is an upper-bound specification from a 1995–2014 sample published in 2020, the other is a pre-Regulation G sample summarized by the authors’ school as “up to” 45%.
- IFRS strategic-report text is the right place to port MD&A tone and going-concern language. It is the wrong place to port the US pension expected-return assumption, which IAS 19 no longer runs through earnings the same way.

### Gaps

- No primary-source magnitude re-extracted for Sloan accruals, Cooper–Gulen–Schill asset growth, Titman–Wei–Xie capex, or Pontiff–Woodgate net issuance. They remain the numeric bar a narrative feature must clear.
- No return paper located for RPO or current RPO, net retention, churn, bookings versus revenue, price versus volume, organic versus acquired, warranty text, lease text, auditor changes, restatement long-run drift, buyback completion versus announcement language, or qualitative goodwill headroom. Absence is this search, not proof of a zero coefficient.
- Lang and Stice-Lawrence (textual boilerplate in annual reports) and Campbell, Chen, Dhaliwal, Lu, and Steele (risk-factor content) were not re-read. Risk-factor **changes** enter only through Cohen, Malloy, and Nguyen.
- Post-2020 out-of-sample returns for Lazy Prices, and post-Regulation G returns for exclusion hedges, were not found.
- The backlog-change 13.7% document did not yield a confirmed author line on the pages read.

## What is incremental to numeric Compustat factors?

### Takeaway

Reading the filing changes the forecast, on the published evidence, when the text is a **change or a residual**: MD&A tone change after earnings surprises and accruals, learned forward-looking tone that turns off accrual mispricing, abnormal press-release tone after a fundamentals model, 10-K/10-Q wording changes that are not a Compustat item, material-weakness disclosure after accruals and distress controls, and non-GAAP “other exclusions” after special items. Restating DSO, inventory, capitalization, F-score lines, asset growth, or pension funded status in sentences does not, in these papers, add a return the ratio does not already have.

### Cited Findings

- MD&A tone change (positive minus negative word-class change versus the prior 10-Q or 10-K) is associated with the short-window filing return after accruals and earnings surprises, and with portfolio drift from two days after filing through one day after the next quarter’s earnings announcement. Part of the drift is prediction of the next earnings surprise; not all of it. The incremental effect is weaker when the information environment is stronger. — [Feldman, Govindaraj, Livnat, and Segal, RAS, 2010](https://ideas.repec.org/a/spr/reaccs/v15y2010i4d10.1007_s11142-009-9111-x.html)
- Forward-looking MD&A tone from a naive Bayes model predicts future earnings after other determinants, and the accrual-return relation disappears when that tone contradicts the accrual. Dictionaries do not. This is the direct “model beats keyword count” result, and it is incremental to the accrual factor rather than a new factor with its own published spread. — [Li, JAR, 2010](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=1623943)
- Abnormal tone is the residual of tone on quantitative performance, risk, and complexity, so by construction it is the piece of the earnings release Compustat fundamentals do not linear-span. It predicts lower future earnings and cash flows and a sign flip in returns (up at announcement, down over the next one to two quarters). — [Huang, Teoh, and Zhang](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=1960376)
- Document similarity is not a Compustat field. Changers’ underperformance survives the authors’ controls in the sense that they tie it to later earnings, news, and bankruptcy rather than to a filing-day jump. They do not, in the abstract, report a horse race against a full q-factor or fundamental-factor model, so “incremental to asset growth and profitability” is only partly tested. — [Cohen, Malloy, and Nguyen](https://doi.org/10.1111/jofi.12885)
- Material-weakness drift survives accruals, momentum, distress, PEAD, and penny stocks. That is incremental to the main numeric fundamental factors named in the user’s question, over a two-quarter horizon, in low-institutional-ownership names. — [Ghosh and coauthors, JBFA, 2026](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jbfa.70012)
- M-score’s return is incremental **inside** the accrual sort: the −19.8% one-year size-adjusted spread is within the lowest accrual quintile. The inputs are still ratios. — [Beneish, Lee, and Nichols](https://cpb-us-w2.wpmucdn.com/sites.udel.edu/dist/a/855/files/2020/07/Earnings-Manipulation-and-Expected-Returns.pdf)
- Exclusions’ return “persists after controlling for various risk factors and other anomalies,” and other exclusions are not the same object as Compustat special items. — [Doyle, Lundholm, and Soliman abstract](https://ideas.repec.org/a/spr/reaccs/v8y2003i2d10.1023_a1024472210359.html)
- Off-balance-sheet pension funded status and the PBO predict returns; the recognized piece does not. After SFAS 158 much of that difference moved onto the balance sheet, so the incremental footnote gap is era-dependent. — [Picconi](https://publications.aaahq.org/accounting-review/article/81/4/925/2885/The-Perils-of-Pensions-Does-Pension-Accounting)
- Customer-concentration changes and backlog changes are incremental to the level of the same series and, in the concentration slides, to size. They are still numbers. The prose helps only if it reveals a relationship change the customer file misses. — [Patatoukas slides](https://pdfs.semanticscholar.org/presentation/7d4b/a069dd746f61c91f25e87457b802d155799e.pdf)
- Constraining-word frequency is nearly uncorrelated with size and age constraint indexes and predicts liquidity events those indexes miss. Incremental for events; return increment not shown. — [Bodnaruk, Loughran, and McDonald](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2331544)
- MD&A going-concern text is incremental to ratios, the market, and the auditor opinion for bankruptcy prediction. Return increment not shown. — [Mayew, Sethuraman, and Venkatachalam](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2272463)

### Inferences

- The practical test for a candidate feature is whether the score still predicts the next return after Sloan accruals, asset growth, profitability, a distress proxy, and the latest earnings surprise. On the evidence above, tone residuals, document-change measures, other-exclusion amounts, and material-weakness indicators are the ones already close to that test. Beneish helps only as a forensic overlay on firms that look accrual-clean.
- Keyword counts of “challenging,” “may,” or “adjusted” will double-count the numeric state of the firm. The papers that find incremental text either residualize tone on fundamentals, restrict to forward-looking sentences, or measure the edit versus last period.

### Gaps

- No sourced regression shows Lazy Prices alpha inside a model that already includes accruals, asset growth, profitability, and distress together.
- No sourced test shows an LLM embedding of MD&A beating Feldman-style tone change or Cohen-style similarity in a return regression. That increment is a hypothesis.
- Proxy CD&A “unearned shares” / performance-grant disclosures predict subsequent returns and are not spanned by guidance, 10-K sentiment, insider sales, or analyst forecasts, but they sit outside 10-K / 10-Q / Item 2.02. Noted so they are not mistaken for an MD&A result. — [ScienceDirect, 2023](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0165410122000428)

## Where is the incremental information in 10-Qs and earnings releases, and how fast does it decay?

### Takeaway

Non-GAAP exclusions and abnormal tone hit in the earnings release and are the fastest fundamental surprises, but the exclusion mispricing in the classic study lasted years, while abnormal-tone reversal is one to two quarters. MD&A tone change in the later 10-Q or 10-K still moves prices after earnings are known, and that incremental drift mostly finishes at the next earnings announcement. The annual 10-K adds a slower, inattention-driven drift from document changes, complexity, pension footnotes, and going-concern text, on the order of several quarters to a year, with pension and UK going-concern results stretching longer. Material-weakness drift is about two quarters. Several of the longest-horizon results are also the most sample-specific.

### Cited Findings

- Earnings release / 8-K Item 2.02: exclusion hedges are formed at the earnings announcement and earn abnormal returns “in the years following,” with the three-year comparison in the school summary. The announcement return already moves against large exclusions, but not by enough. Regime break: Regulation G is after the 1988–1999 sample. — [Michigan summary of Doyle, Lundholm, and Soliman](https://www.bus.umich.edu/FacultyResearch/InTheNews/Archive/ProFormaEarnings.htm)
- Abnormal tone: positive at the release, negative over the next one and two quarters. Decay of the mispricing is inside two quarters. — [Huang, Teoh, and Zhang](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=1960376)
- 10-Q and 10-K MD&A tone change: drift window is explicitly two days after the SEC filing through one day after the **next quarter’s** preliminary earnings announcement. Incremental information of the periodic MD&A, beyond the earnings number, is largely resolved within one quarter. Short-window filing returns show some immediate incorporation as well. — [Feldman et al.](https://ideas.repec.org/a/spr/reaccs/v15y2010i4d10.1007_s11142-009-9111-x.html)
- 10-K complexity drift lasts about 12 months and is larger for complex filings. Immediate filing-window returns already contain information about future profitability, so the 10-K is not ignored; it is incompletely priced. — [You and Zhang](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11142-008-9083-2)
- Document changes (quarterly and annual filings): no announcement effect. Returns arrive only as later news, earnings, or events reveal what the edit meant. That is slower than tone-change drift and is the argument for reading the 10-K even when the earnings release was uneventful. The extreme portfolio is described in monthly alpha, so the horizon is multi-month, not a one-day filing trade. — [Cohen, Malloy, and Nguyen](https://doi.org/10.1111/jofi.12885)
- MD&A modification’s three-day filing reaction weakened over the 2000s as modifications themselves declined. Whatever incremental pricing the market did at the 10-K, it did less of it later in that sample. — [Brown and Tucker](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=1452977)
- Material weakness: negligible at announcement, negative drift over the next two quarters (60 and 120 trading days). — [Ghosh and coauthors](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jbfa.70012)
- Pension footnote changes: not in price or in analyst forecasts at the 10-K; incorporated as subsequent quarterly earnings reveal the earnings effect. Underfunding return gaps in the earlier literature are described over horizons up to five years, with the distress critique above. — [Picconi](https://publications.aaahq.org/accounting-review/article/81/4/925/2885/The-Perils-of-Pensions-Does-Pension-Accounting); [Franzoni and Marín](https://ideas.repec.org/p/upf/upfgen/871.html)
- Going concern: UK underperformance measured over 12 months after publication. US lottery-stock underperformance also measured over 12 months. A press re-release of an already filed US going-concern opinion still produces abnormal volatility and small-trade volume, which is evidence that part of the audience has not processed the 10-K. — [Taffler, Lu, and Kausar](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0165410104000813); [2026 gambling paper](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1386418125000515); [re-release paper, Accounting and Business Research](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/00014788.2016.1255586)
- Customer-concentration change: one-year-ahead returns, with a disproportionate share clustered at subsequent earnings announcements, so the decay path is quarterly earnings, not a smooth year. — [Patatoukas slides](https://pdfs.semanticscholar.org/presentation/7d4b/a069dd746f61c91f25e87457b802d155799e.pdf)
- Backlog change: the 13.7% hedge is “the year after” portfolio formation. Backlog level mispricing is “future” abnormal returns without a tighter window in the abstract. — [backlog-change PDF](https://scispace.com/pdf/changes-in-order-backlog-and-future-returns-8l5w7mlk5r.pdf); [Rajgopal, Shevlin, and Venkatachalam](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1023/A:1027364031775)
- UTB additions: longer post-10-K drift and wider spreads, i.e. an annual-footnote processing delay. Length not quantified. — [Small and Song](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3815951)
- Filing-date negative-word returns (Loughran and McDonald) and filing-date “substantial doubt” returns decay inside the filing window by construction. They are not quarterly holding-period signals. — [Loughran and McDonald, JF, 2011](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.2010.01625.x); [Barron’s red-flag paper](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=1510188)

### Inferences

- For a live model, the earnings release should be scored for exclusion mix and abnormal tone the day it is furnished; the 10-Q MD&A should be scored for tone change and material-weakness language when it files, with an expected life of about one quarter for tone and about two quarters for a weakness. The 10-K should be diffed against the prior 10-K for MD&A, litigation, risk factors, executives, pension assumptions, UTB rollforward, and going-concern sentences, with an expected life of several quarters and no assumption of a day-1 jump.
- Fast decay (one quarter) is the pattern when the text is a signal about the next earnings surprise. Slow decay is the pattern when the text is easy to skip (long 10-K, footnote pensions, going concern in lottery names). Those are different trades.
- Do not stack a 10-K tone score on top of the earnings-release tone score without a difference. Feldman is explicitly the change from the prior periodic filing, which may mostly repeat the release.

### Gaps

- No paper located measures the return to 10-Q footnote items (RPO update, contingency update, covenant language) in the window after the earnings release and before the 10-Q, versus after the 10-Q itself.
- Griffin’s “Got Information?” filing-window study was not re-opened; filing-window facts here come from You and Zhang, Brown and Tucker, Loughran and McDonald, and Feldman et al.
- Decay after 2014 for document-change alphas, and after 2003 for exclusion alphas, is not in the sources read.
